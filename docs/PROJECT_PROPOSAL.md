Title: SphereCast: Spherical Dynamics-enhanced Test-Time Calibration for Medium-range Weather Forecasting

Description: Accurate medium-range weather forecasting is critical to a wide range of industries, including renewable energy, agriculture, aviation, maritime transportation, insurance, and emergency management. Reliable forecasts help power-grid operators balance weather-dependent generation, enable logistics providers to reduce disruption, support climate-sensitive business planning, and strengthen preparedness for high-impact events. Recent AI weather models have substantially improved forecasting accuracy and efficiency. However, models deployed in real-world systems inevitably encounter seasonal variation, evolving atmospheric regimes, systematic regional biases, and discrepancies between training data and operational inputs. Continuously retraining these large models is expensive, while leaving them unchanged prevents them from learning from newly verified forecast errors. A lightweight and reliable mechanism for adapting forecasts at test time is therefore essential for turning AI weather prediction models into sustainable industrial services.

Test-time calibration has shown promise in classification and general spatio-temporal prediction, but has not been systematically studied for weather and climate forecasting. Directly applying existing calibration strategies is inadequate because atmospheric fields evolve on a sphere, exhibit strongly coupled spatial scales, and contain variable- and horizon-dependent errors. Moreover, forecast observations become available only after their corresponding valid times, creating delayed supervision that must be handled without future-information leakage. Aggressive online optimization can overfit a small number of recent events and degrade an already strong backbone, whereas grid-based corrections may introduce artificial longitudinal boundaries, distort polar regions, or fail to capture coherent planetary-scale errors.

To address this gap, this project proposes SphereCast, centered on SphereTTC, the first test-time calibration framework specifically designed for weather and climate forecasting. SphereTTC represents global forecast fields in a truncated spherical-harmonic space, where geographically coherent errors can be modeled according to their natural spatial frequencies. It estimates conservative affine corrections using only previously verified prediction–observation pairs, with recency weighting to track evolving conditions and robust weighting to reduce the influence of anomalous events. A chronological validation mechanism evaluates whether each proposed correction generalizes to more recent forecasts, while risk-aware gates and a no-operation option suppress unreliable adaptation. Multi-timescale and seasonal calibration experts capture complementary short-term, persistent, and periodic error structures. When multiple forecast providers are available, SphereTTC can further learn lead- and variable-specific constrained combinations with trusted reference systems while preserving the contribution of the target backbone. Since the forecasting model remains frozen, SphereTTC can be attached to heterogeneous operational systems with limited computational and maintenance overhead.

Building on the same spherical principle, this project further introduces SphereDyn as a geometry-aware forecasting backbone that strengthens the predictions supplied to SphereTTC. SphereDyn learns a parameter-shared daily flow operator and combines spherical local modeling, truncated spherical-spectral dynamics, semi-Lagrangian transport, and harmonic climate tendencies. A direct observation-time pathway extracts motion information from recent atmospheric states, while a multi-resolution spherical residual pathway connects regional structures with planetary circulation. SphereDyn and SphereTTC therefore address two complementary stages of the forecasting pipeline: SphereDyn learns atmospheric evolution before deployment, and SphereTTC continually corrects systematic errors after deployment using causally available feedback.

Extensive experiments on global medium-range forecasting show that SphereTTC consistently improves forecast accuracy and anomaly correlation across diverse variables, horizons, and forecasting backbones, while the integration with SphereDyn provides an effective end-to-end spherical forecasting system. By establishing test-time calibration as a new research direction for weather and climate forecasting, SphereCast enables forecasting services to improve from operational feedback without repeatedly updating large backbone models. It offers a practical foundation for adaptive global weather intelligence and makes accurate forecasting easier to maintain, integrate, and deploy across industrial decision systems.
